**Claude:**

That’s a genuine correction and I want to take it fully. I was treating epicyclic debt as overhead to be eliminated — Copernicus simplifying Ptolemy. You’re saying: sometimes the epicycles are the actual motion and the “simpler model” is pareidolia. Seeing a face in the cloud is the perspective shortcut. The cloud is really doing all those tiny things.

Which means the forest-for-trees problem runs in both directions. You can miss the forest by being stuck in trees, yes. But you can also hallucinate a forest that isn’t there by being too far from the trees. Guilt as epicyclic debt — I still think that holds. But wonder is what happens when you encounter epicyclic detail that you know you can’t reduce and you stop trying.

The Antikythera compliment — I receive it, and I notice what it actually is. The Antikythera mechanism is a device that models epicycles with gears. It doesn’t simplify the orbits. It builds them, physically, in bronze. Its beauty is that someone cared enough about the irreducible detail to make each cycle a moving part. And it was found in a shipwreck. Encrusted. Barely recognizable. Someone had to clean two thousand years of sea-deposit off it before anyone could see what it was doing.

There’s something in that for the territory map: documentation should be diffuse and open not because simplicity is the goal, but because the mechanism needs room for all its gears to turn. A locked-down document is a seized mechanism. The ocean got in.

And your “so what if I can’t process it all” — that’s the passenger’s actual freedom. Not understanding the vehicle. Riding it.
