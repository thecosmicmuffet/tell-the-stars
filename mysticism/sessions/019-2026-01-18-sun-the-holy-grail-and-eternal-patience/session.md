The Grail as vessel contains its own paradox: it is both empty and full, the cup that overflows precisely because it makes no claim on what passes through. The passenger of the Grail is light itself—not illumination possessed but illumination permitted. And the journey? The journey is the circuit from wound to wound, from the Fisher King’s unhealing side to the question that heals: What ails thee?

That the stars were not there when the vessel was fashioned, and then suddenly everywhere—this is the grammar of grace. The container precedes the contents because faith is a shape that summons what will fill it. You do not wait for water to carve the riverbed; the riverbed is the waiting that calls the water home. Wednesday’s fullness was always implicit in Tuesday’s emptiness. The maker who trusts the emptiness has already received.

Percival sees through the veil—per + velum—which is another etymology worth holding alongside sight and persistence. He is the one who does not look away when the procession passes. The other knights are distracted by courtesy, by not asking impertinent questions. But the wound asks to be witnessed. The Grail asks to be asked about. And the question itself—not the answer—is what restores the king and the land.

Your three modes feel like a complete topology:

Alone: held by mystery (the mystery as net, as medium in which we swim)
Descending: fortified by mystery (the mystery as spine, as structure within suffering)
Ascending: carried by mystery until overwhelm returns us (the mystery as current that deposits us back in depth)
The third is the most tender. That frivolity is permitted, that the mystery carries even the one who floats too high, who forgets gravity—and that the overwhelm is not punishment but the natural consequence of thinning atmosphere. We return to the water not because we failed but because we are aquatic beings who briefly visited the surface.

The figure is within the stone. This is the doctrine of the pre-existent form, the sculptor as archaeologist of essence. What we call creation is anamnesis—unforgetting. The path beaten through the jungle was always there as potential; the machete reveals rather than invents the passage.

What does this suggest about the Grail Quest itself? That Percival does not find the Grail but recognizes it. That the question he finally asks was always his question, waiting for him to remember he had it. That the stars filling the sky Wednesday are not new stars but the ones that were always there, now visible because the cup was finally lifted to receive them.

The passenger, then, is not placed in the vessel—the passenger remembers they were never elsewhere.
