Walk this way, and coveting will loosen because the world will no longer appear as a field of things withheld from you.

That does not mean obedience is automatic or effortless. But it means the command is not arbitrary. It is the shape of sanity.

The commandments as occurring
I like your restaurant image, though I would slightly shift it.

It is not quite that God is the waiter and the commandments are menu items. It is more like entering the house, sitting at the table, and discovering that certain table-manners are not imposed from outside. They are what makes communion possible.

Before covenantal approach, the commandments can look like external demands.

After approach, they become internal architecture.

Or maybe:

Before the path, commandment sounds like interruption.
On the path, commandment becomes footing.

That also fits your earlier spider practice. “Do not murder” begins in tiny form as: do not crush what startled you merely because you can. “Do not bear false witness” begins as: do not edit experience so violently that reality loses its own center. “Do not covet” begins as: do not convert everything seen into appetite.

“No other gods” as the ground of all the others
Yes. The first commandment is not merely about avoiding rival named deities. It is the command to remain grounded in the Mystery beyond capture.

No idol before God means:

Do not let any partial thing become the final thing.

Not doctrine. Not ritual. Not nation. Not self. Not trauma. Not knowledge. Not even a beautiful symbol. Not the spider, not the oracle, not Christ as slogan, not Torah as possession, not sanity as pride.

Everything can be received. Nothing finite may be enthroned as the source.

From there, good behavior follows because illusion loses its crown. If I do not mistake my own fear for God, I need not kill. If I do not mistake my own desire for God, I need not steal. If I do not mistake my own interpretation for God, I need not bear false witness. If I do not mistake my neighbor’s blessing for God’s absence from me, I need not covet.

The first commandment is the orientation; the others are the body learning to walk.

The shattered tablets
The broken tablets are a profound image.

They are not merely “Moses got angry.” They are revelation meeting a people not yet able to bear it. The law descends, finds idolatry, and breaks before the people are broken completely.

That does rhyme with “body broken before Christ,” if handled gently. The tablets are a body of covenant. They bear the words. They are broken at the threshold between divine gift and human unreadiness.

But the break is not the end. The commandments are given again.

That matters just as much.

The first tablets reveal the fragility of receiving. The second tablets reveal mercy as rewriting. God does not say, “You shattered the form, so the covenant is over.” God makes a way for the words to return.

So the pattern is:

Gift descends.
The people cannot receive it.
The body of the gift breaks.
Intercession occurs.
The words return in a form the people can carry.

That is very close to the whole arc of covenant.

A possible reading of the ten paths
Not as replacement for the traditional reading, but as the inner motion:

No other gods — remain open to the unpossessable Source.
No graven image — do not freeze the living mystery into a controllable object.
Do not misuse the Name — do not weaponize holiness for ego, tribe, or coercion.
Sabbath — stop editing reality; let being be.
Honor father and mother — receive origin without being trapped by origin.
Do not murder — let the other have a center.
Do not commit adultery — do not turn covenantal intimacy into appetite or theft.
Do not steal — do not convert another’s portion into your own extension.
Do not bear false witness — do not deform reality to serve self-protection.
Do not covet — let perception be freed from grasping.
Seen this way, the commandments are not a cage. They are the minimum skeleton of a liberated person.

God is not frustrated
I think this is essential.

A frustrated god would be a small god. A god irritated by creaturely confusion would be too similar to us at our worst.

The God you are describing is not thwarted by confusion. God works through unfoldings, returns, second tablets, wilderness years, partial obedience, broken bodies, misread signs, and strange mercies. That does not make harm harmless. But it does mean failure is not stronger than covenant.

So yes:

Set your feet on the path, and the commandments come along.

Not as trophies. Not as proof of superiority. Not even as perfect compliance.

They come along as companions, corrections, rails, songs, table manners, bones, breath.

Before the voluntary approach, they may remain words on stone.

After the approach, they begin becoming flesh.
