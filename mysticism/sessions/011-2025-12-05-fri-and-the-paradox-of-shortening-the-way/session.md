Yes — your tension is the right one.

קְפִיצַת הַדֶּרֶךְ / qefitzat ha-derekh is usually rendered “the shortening of the way.” It can sound like “fast travel,” but the older image is subtler: not merely that the traveler accelerates, but that the road/earth contracts. Jastrow gives קָפַץ as “to contract, shrink; to close,” and Rashi reads Genesis 24:42 as Eliezer leaving and arriving the same day because “the earth shrank for him.” 

So there are two versions:

Profane shortening: impatience, domination, “skip the process,” spiritual venture capital, acceleration as refusal of ripening.

Sacred shortening: the path contracts because the traveler, task, timing, and destination have aligned. The self is not stronger; resistance is lower. The way has not been cheated. It has become contiguous.

That matters for מֶרְכָּבָה / merkavah, the chariot. In Jewish tradition, Ma‘aseh Merkabah is the lore of the heavenly throne-chariot, especially around Ezekiel 1 and 10; the Mishnah treats it as dangerous material, not to be expounded except to one who is already wise and able to understand from within. 
 The Ezekiel image is not a cart being driven across space like a wagon. It is a vehicle of aligned motion: creatures, wings, wheels within wheels, spirit in the wheels, motion “without turning.” 

So the two concepts fit like this:

The merkavah is the structure of right motion.
Kefitzat ha-derekh is what happens to distance when right motion is achieved.

That makes “shortening” less like willful acceleration and more like topological mercy. The chariot does not conquer the road. The road recognizes the chariot.

Herbert’s Kwisatz Haderach is very close to this phrase; Jewish Book Council notes the relation between Kefitzat ha-derekh, “the Shortening of the Way,” and Herbert’s “Kwisatz Haderach,” the one who can be “many places at once.” 
 In Dune, the danger is that prescience turns the shortened way into a trap: the one who sees the path may become imprisoned by the path. The messiah becomes sin-eater because he metabolizes everyone else’s deferred consequences. He is not merely “accelerated”; he is condensed.

That gives your masculine/feminine reading teeth. The feminine initiates the program of discernment, breeding, memory, hidden transmission; the masculine recipient becomes the place where the contradiction rests, painfully embodied. But the error would be to conclude that one controls and the other suffers. The deeper puzzle is mutual: each believes it has surrendered more than the other, because each experiences surrender from inside its own wound.

Friday/day six then becomes not merely “synthesis,” but the creation of the one who can name and therefore bind without possessing. Adam as namer is created at the edge of Sabbath: speech, distinction, partnership, animality, vocation, loneliness, completion, exhaustion. Then sundown comes.
