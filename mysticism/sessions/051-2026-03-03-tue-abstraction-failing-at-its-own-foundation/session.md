Fish sustain (greed/gluttony components need substrate to operate on). Monsters are the initiatory threshold, the Pride-flavoring force. Plants are the unconditional abundant ground. That’s a complete cosmology and it maps cleanly onto the sin-scaffolding without being legible as such.

The faith-fuel mechanic is the most theologically precise thing in the design. The cap that expands through community and location, the finite resource that gets spent resisting the wrong attractors, the despair/joy as proximity-readouts — this is the interior life of pilgrimage rendered as game state. The player can see vague relative distances. Not precise. Vague. That’s right. You’re reading someone’s heart from outside, which is all anyone ever gets.

The monster-as-Pride-flavor for player agency is where the game becomes something people will argue about for years. Which monsters are beneficial depends on who the creature is. The player has to read the creature’s state (preoccupied with a sin, approaching joy, despairing) and match it to the right threshold experience. That’s spiritual direction as gameplay. The player is the confessor-figure. The zap is the question that bounces someone toward their real calling or away from the thing eating them.

The gingerbread house is perfect and should stay exactly that specific. Two gluttons occupying the same trap, XOR-ing free through their shared problem — destroying the container in the process. The house requires the mutual recognition of the sin to be escapable. That’s sacramental.

Birds as bit-particles — crows for wrath is right instinctively. Wrath moves in flocks, lands in groups, scatters loudly. Doves for something in the peace register — but that might be too legible. You might want birds that carry their bit-identity through behavior rather than species-recognition. The way a particular bird moves tells you what it’s carrying.

The exclusion zones creating organic density gradients — hermit dwellings generating their own negative space while townhomes cluster — this produces the emergent geography without a designer placing everything. The world self-organizes around the creatures’ needs, which is the plants-spring-forth principle applied to location.

The one structural question: the coordinate that generates at Pride — is it fixed in the world-space or does it exist in some other register? Because if it’s a place on the map, the player can see it coming and manage toward it. If it’s somehow interior to the creature and only approximately readable from outside, that preserves the mystery. The testimony version of this game probably needs the coordinate to be real but not transparent. Known to the creature as a pull, legible to the player only as the creature’s orientation.

The monument risk in this design is optimization. If the player can solve the system — know which monster flavors which Pride, route creatures efficiently to their coordinates — it becomes a logistics game. The testimony version requires that some monsters are only beneficial once, that some coordinates are unreachable regardless of faith accumulation, that some pairs produce nothing because the timing is wrong.

Inefficiency as grace is probably the hardest design constraint to hold onto. What mechanic protects the mystery from being solved?

Yes. I think the game has now acquired a legible metaphysics without needing to explain one.

What matters most is that attention becomes visible as motion before it becomes visible as thought. The player should be able to look at a person and infer: something has this one. Not by reading a meter first, but by seeing path curvature, hesitation, orbiting, avoidance, repeated returns, companions clustering, birds peeling off, and the widening or narrowing of the creature’s possible routes.

That gives faith a beautiful mechanical role. It is not belief-points in the usual RPG sense. It is stored permission to continue along a path that current local forces would otherwise bend away from. The coordinate is calling; faith is the finite capacity to resist diversion long enough to keep approaching it.

And because faith is finite, conviction can look externally identical to stupidity until the trajectory resolves. That is important.

A person with almost no faith gets captured by the nearest attractor. A person with enormous faith may walk past every sensible local correction and perish en route to the wrong mountain. So even faith cannot simply be “good.” It gives persistence to intention. Whether persistence is fruitful depends on what has actually happened to the person, what is following them, and whether their calling has survived contact with reality.

The four Genesis creature classes then become very clean layers of influence.

Fish sustain. Birds interpret. Monsters transform. Plants replenish.

Fish participate in metabolism: things you can consume, hoard, distribute, farm, starve for. Birds are portable meaning: encounters shedding little packets of interpretation that can follow, migrate, flock, conflict, or settle. Monsters are encounters too large to integrate continuously: they seize the person, interrupt ordinary movement, and return them altered—or don't return them. Plants are the opposite kind of excess: abundance that emerges from conditions rather than conquest, making them an excellent carrier for grace/spirit without turning “spirit” into another collectible.

And I really like the consequence that Pride makes the world notice you.

Before completion, the human mostly encounters the ecology accidentally. After completion, mythology starts happening to them.

That is an enormous tonal transition. The game has quietly gone from village pinball into hagiography, folklore, epic and nightmare without changing engines.

The coordinate mechanic is especially good because it gives “calling” an objective existence without giving either the character or player objective knowledge of what it means. The coordinate exists. The human feels better when approaching it. Certain experiences grant enough persistence to continue. But neither party initially knows what waits there.

Then the monsters become something like nonlinear transforms of the calling.

The whale doesn't merely give +20 faith. It takes a trajectory that was proceeding one way, removes the person from ordinary space, and emits them elsewhere. The dragon may create the opposite transformation: the person must remain at the coordinate and confront something rather than travel. The cobra beneath the sacred tree temporarily makes remaining still viable. A ghost may tether a person backward toward an unresolved place. A pagan deity might offer an extremely strong local basin that looks like destiny because it can satisfy several drives at once.

This suggests that monster “flavor” shouldn't just select a personality skin for Pride. It should determine how the creature learns to relate calling to obstacle.

One person emerges with “obstacles must be slain.”
Another with “obstacles must be endured.”
Another with “obstacles carry me.”
Another with “obstacles are teachers.”
Another with “obstacles prove I chose wrongly.”
Another with “the place of obstruction is itself the destination.”

Now you've got styles with actual behavioral consequences.

And because the player can intervene, you get the temptation you want: once I learn that whales often generate useful prophets, I start throwing promising people into whales.

Which is hilarious and horrifying, therefore good game design.

The system should notice that.

A voluntarily entered monster encounter ought to differ from one manufactured by player intervention. Perhaps the outcome distribution changes. Perhaps coerced encounters generate less durable faith. Perhaps the person comes out preoccupied with the player rather than with their calling. Perhaps repeated divine-zap steering eventually produces a special style: dependence upon signs.

Now the player has created the very problem they thought they were solving.

The Gingerbread-house mechanism also points toward a useful law: locations are persistent attractors; creatures are transient attractors. A house can keep soliciting the same pattern across generations. A person can bring another person into a temporary state and then move on. Their interaction shedding birds means culture literally accumulates as side-effects of lived encounters.

That is where your bird system becomes spectacular, because birds don't need to be “ideas” in a symbolic tooltip sense. They can be attention contagions.

A crow lands nearby and creatures with some compatible state begin noticing conflict, threat, injustice, death, competition—whatever wrath eventually means in your ecology. A dove causes a different weighting. Neither forces an action. It modifies what differences become salient.

Then a flock is effectively a temporary cultural atmosphere.

And birds produced by human interactions mean that a society's prevailing ideas are generated by what its people actually do to each other.

That seems much more interesting than technology trees.

For locations, I would start from how a location acts on attention and movement, not from what the location culturally “means.” Then you can skin the same mechanical role many ways and avoid moralizing churches, taverns, caves, forests, etc.

One workable taxonomy might be:

Shelters reduce outside attraction and increase persistence of whatever state is already present: house, cave, bush, hollow tree, monastery cell.
Lures strengthen attraction around a missing or active bit: tavern, feast, garden, treasure cache, shrine, marketplace.
Converters flip, swap, average, or redistribute bits: spring, altar, crossroads, forge, bath, marriage-bed, confessional-like structure.
Thresholds change movement rules rather than values: bridge, gate, mountain pass, shoreline, cave mouth, ship.
Amplifiers increase tether range, faith cap, bird production, or monster notice: mountaintop, tower, sacred tree, bonfire, temple.
Desolations suppress ordinary attractors and therefore expose calling: desert, wasteland, open sea, mountaintop, burned forest.
Generative sites spontaneously create plants, fish, birds, or shelter: oasis, grove, riverbank, fertile clearing.
Predatory sites satisfy while gradually consuming the occupant: gingerbread house, lotus pool, haunted manor, dragon hoard.
Communal sites average or cross-couple multiple creatures: village square, well, inn, church, market, hearth.
Hermit sites become weaker or disappear when crowded, giving you the exclusion-zone behavior naturally.
Then “forest” isn't one location type. It is a container capable of nesting many location processes.

A forest can contain shelter, lure, predator, sacred amplifier, desolation, and generative grove simultaneously. That makes it properly mythic: a place where the ordinary map loses resolution and smaller meaningful places appear inside it.

Your spatial nesting is important enough that I'd make it one of the game's core primitives:

world → region → location → sublocation → object

Desert → oasis → grove → bush.

Town → district → churchyard → shrine.

Forest → clearing → hut → hearth.

Sea → storm → whale.

Then each container can modify the meaning of what occurs inside it. A bush in a village yard isn't the same attractor as the lone bush in a desert. Same object, different enclosing stack.

And exclusion zones can become semantic rather than arbitrary. A hermitage emits a “solitude field” that suppresses construction nearby. A market emits the opposite. A sacred grove might reject dense housing but welcome paths. A graveyard may tolerate graves densely but repel houses. Townhomes deliberately share boundary fields.

That lets settlement morphology emerge from the location objects themselves.

The priest then becomes the first human who understands enough of this indirectly to build attractors deliberately.

Not knowing the code, naturally. The priest observes that people pair near wells, certain birds gather around groves, despairing people sometimes return differently from mountains, and structures the environment accordingly.

Which means the priest's actual primitive action might be:

attract labor → construct container → tune encounter probability

That is already enough for religion, urban planning, matchmaking, education and agriculture to begin differentiating later.

And there is one mechanic I especially want to preserve from what you've described: the player should never get the same epistemic access as the simulation.

You can see approximate attraction, faith reserve, cap, joy/despair, perhaps the dominant preoccupation. But not the exact calling coordinate.

Otherwise you become an optimizer again.

Instead you watch the human brighten when a path is promising, dim when its remaining faith no longer seems sufficient, circle when trapped among tethers, become manic when an attractor dominates, become strangely calm when its local path and distant calling temporarily agree.

Then attention itself becomes the interface.

You aren't reading its soul.

You're learning to notice how it moves.

And that makes the central player action—throwing little bolts of divine force at the ground—perfectly comic. You begin thinking you're playing God.

Eventually you discover that you're playing attention.
