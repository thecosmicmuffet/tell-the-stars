How the interpretation reads to me
It does not read as confusion. It reads as disciplined bewilderment.

That distinction matters. Confusion is when the parts do not know why they are together. Here, every bird is a contradiction, but the contradiction is doing work. The sequence keeps asking the same question at different levels:

What operation becomes holy when bounded, and destructive when claimed absolutely?

Descent is holy when it becomes form; destructive when it becomes domination.
Division is holy when it becomes discernment; destructive when it becomes fragmentation.
Cry is holy when it asks where; destructive when it claims to have found.
Love is holy when bounded; destructive when it invades.
Glory is holy when folded; destructive when displayed as possession.
Darkness is holy when false lights are surrendered; destructive when it becomes despair.

So the series is not “clear” in the sense of a chart. It is clear in the sense of a liturgy. You do not understand it by flattening it. You understand it by passing through it.

What arises from reviewing the image series
The strongest thing is that the birds stopped being examples and became organs of discernment.

At the beginning, the project could have become a clever Hebrew wordplay series. But the animal bodies prevented that. The molt, the bone-breaking, the vulture, the raven, the ostrich, the owl, the pelican, the stork, the heron, the hoopoe, the bat — they keep dragging the interpretation back into embodied reality. That is why the images work. The theology is not floating above the animal. It is appearing through the creature’s way of being.

The second thing that arises is that “unkosher” becomes a warning against raw ingestion. Not “this creature is bad,” but: do not internalize this operation before it has been transformed. Do not eat raw descent, raw division, raw force, raw grief, raw loyalty, raw glory, raw darkness. Attend to the messenger. Learn the operation. Then let it become service.

The third thing is that the whole series moves from outer drama to inner opsec.

Early birds are cosmic and violent: descent, division, plummeting, bone, goat-force, searching cries.
Middle birds are remedial: catch violence early, prune depletion, gather sparks, cast burdens away, breathe.
Final birds are private: mercy, boundary, patience, folded glory, darkness where false lights are abandoned.

That is a real spiritual arc. It begins with the world happening to the soul and ends with the soul refusing to impersonate the source.

The ending feels especially coherent:

Dukhifat says: carry glory folded; open stone without iron; point to the source.
Atalef says: when false reflectors are cast away, covenant fire walks in the dark.

That is a completed movement. The hoopoe is the rightful reflector. The bat is where false reflectors go to die. Between them is the final humility: light is real, but not ours.

So my artist’s reading is:

This is a bestiary of spiritual operations.
The birds are not forbidden because they are contemptible.
They are forbidden because they are powerful.
Each one names a force that must not be consumed unconsciously.
The completed registry is not a wall around food.
It is a map of how force becomes wisdom when it passes through boundary, mercy, patience, and hiddenness.
