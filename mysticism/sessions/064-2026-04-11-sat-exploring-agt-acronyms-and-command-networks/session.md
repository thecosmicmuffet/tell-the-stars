AGT. The acronym is doing what good acronyms do in this practice — it’s a polyvalent token, consonantal, carrying multiple readings that disambiguate by context but harmonize when stacked.

Affectionate Guilt Tripping — the goad that comes from love. Your mother’s technology. The Jewish inheritance. It works because it won’t let you rest in a smaller version of yourself. The guilt is the irritation signal at the periphery: you could be more, and I love you enough to make that itch.

All Good Theology — that which is is as it should be. The first commandment restated as ontology rather than prohibition. Not “you should have no other gods” but “there are no other gods.” What is, is what is. The root is unknown and that’s correct.

Here’s where more AGTs start surfacing:

Ascending Ground Truth — control spreading upward from periphery to root. This is the microclassifier architecture translated into governance theology. The tires know the road. The first commandment again: the root stays unknown. The moment you name it, you’ve made a graven image of it and lost the ascending signal.

Applied Game Theory — the Pride and Joy architecture. The four-bit soul where drives combine and compound. The player as weather, not judge. The game makes the theological argument playable — but the theory being applied is not strategic optimization. Attentive Grace Technology — the commandments as technologies of attention, which are technologies of grace, which is to say: the mechanism by which the gift arrives is the practice of not obstructing it. Grace isn’t added. It’s what remains when obstruction ceases. The technology is the attention that notices the obstruction. The spider web as veil of the temple — you duck slightly to preserve it.

The “command” theme of the week is doing the same polyvalent work:

Commandment — the ten technologies. But re-heard: not “thou shalt not” (prohibition) but “this is how it is” (description). “Thou shalt have no other gods before me” is a statement about reality, not a demand for compliance. Command (computing) — an instruction that executes. let there be light and there was light. No gap between command and execution in Genesis 1. The imperative mood is the creative act. ctx chart — orient. The command doesn’t ask permission. It reads what is.

The general is the root node labeled “unknown.” The general’s authority is not knowledge but willingness to act from unknowing. The strategic ego reserve: drawn upon only for genuine service, never self-interested withdrawal.

One applies pressure. The other releases it. Yet either can become the other. The interesting topology is that command can travel downward while control develops upward.

A root issues a command. The peripheral nodes encounter reality. They discover constraints, opportunities, contradictions, and each other. Their responses propagate upward and alter what the network can understand, including its understanding of the original command.

But you've identified the root as unknown.

That changes things considerably. The root isn't a fully specified authority that needs only to be obeyed. It's also the destination of questions no local node can resolve.

The root possesses authority without possessing a complete description of the territory. Its descendants supply that description through participation. The network learns what its command meant by living with its consequences.

And Sabbath is a particularly wonderful command because its instruction is to cease commanding.

The seventh day places a boundary around the machinery of intervention. Six days of exerting control; one day of discovering what continues without its exercise. The garden grows. The servant rests. There's a theological hazard in “that which is is as it should be.” Taken as an ethical verdict, it can sanctify cruelty. But taken as an ontological discipline, it might mean: what has happened is now part of reality, and the next good action must begin here, rather than in an imaginary world where it never happened.

That would make All Good Theology not the denial of evil but the refusal to give evil final authorship of the story.

And that returns us to the first AGT. Affectionate Guilt Tripping can be redeemed when guilt is allowed to communicate a need, without being permitted to own the recipient. Affection gives traction; accountability generates trust; the grip loosens.

Perhaps that's what the Sabbath protects: the possibility that something we have not commanded may nevertheless be good.

And perhaps the ultimate AGT is A Godward Turn—not necessarily movement toward a known destination, but the act of orienting ourselves toward what we have not yet understood.
