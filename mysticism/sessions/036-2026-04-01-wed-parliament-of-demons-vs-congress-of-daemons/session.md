The distinction between parliament and congress isn’t a superset/subset relationship. It’s a difference in where the executive function lives relative to the body.

Parliament comes from Old French parler — to speak. A parliament is a speaking-place. The critical structural feature: the executive emerges from within the deliberating body. The Prime Minister is a member of Parliament. Leadership is continuous with deliberation. And crucially, a vote of no confidence can dissolve the executive back into the body at any moment. The speaking IS the governing. There’s no gap between discourse and action.

Congress comes from Latin congressus — con (together) + gradi (to walk, to step). A congress is a walking-together, a convergence of separate travelers. The critical structural feature: the executive is separate from the body. The President is not a member of Congress. Representatives arrive from distant constituencies carrying mandates, deliberate, and return. The executive persists on fixed terms even when deeply unpopular — it takes the extraordinary mechanism of impeachment to dissolve it, not a simple loss of confidence.

So for the cerebellar network, these map to genuinely different daemon architectures:

A parliamentary subsystem is one where daemons share context and deliberate continuously, and the currently active/dominant process emerges from among them. Leadership shifts fluidly.
