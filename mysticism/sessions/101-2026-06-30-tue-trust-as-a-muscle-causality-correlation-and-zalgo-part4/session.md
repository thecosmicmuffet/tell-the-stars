There's the notion of the 4th wall. As a construct it's interesting because it is presumed--usually a narrative observer boundary, where the reader or viewer of a piece of art or even a non-fiction event purportedly won't alter the event. Yet, all narratives are already downstream of stable iterative forms which inhabit people and bootup in our perception, so there is no 4th wall when the purpose of sensory stimuli, particularly from an artifact like a text, or media, is to instigate change and perpetuate processes. These processes may be co-riders in a bundled package like genomic concept or maybe they're cybernetic invitations of handles and products for use which are competing for energy in the space of the embodied life of the person. In any case, it's not necessarily the case that there's a wall and it's not necessarily the case that the purported features of the media are the literal function. If we look at it mechanistically, the effect on the purpose is achieved by whatever can achieve it, whether it's disjointed confusion or highly ordered and supposedly legible as 'real'. I took a history degree as my Bachelor's and, in the final analysis, it's all not only narrative, but narrative whose purpose is anything *but* the 'real' view of the past. Instead, views of the past are like views of distant objects. I would be able to give you details about objects and scenery within 100 ft that I can't give you at 1000 ft, and once things get to a mile, my ability to understand what I'm looking at makes my descriptions take on a form that is primarily about the distance and not about the content of what I can see. In this way History is the same set of stories told about 50, 100, 300, 500, 1000 and so on years ago, until it hits a vague area where something like 3000-15000 years is one blurry band of 'the way things were practically forever' and then beyond this are highly conceptual bands like 50000 years ago, in which human behavior is canonically assumed to be 'noble savagery' with paradoxical assumptions of talented artifact creation mixed with inadequate creativity--and homogenous--all the tribes of humanity are locked in step with the same knowledge base. The result is an inability to look at meta-generations of cycles of civilizational building and collapse. It may very well be that the process of booting up language in humans was a community-sized back-propagation puzzle with discussion between parents and children enabling language to better stabilize. Knowing how modern people sometimes freeze their understandings and achieve only humble offerings, it may be that the true invisible hand of human development may have been the slow accumulation of cross-culture artisan guilds or traditions. In any case, the 4th wall looks like a psychological barrier that pretends to be aloof from what's being seen--in point of fact, a fully immersed and participatory situation. When looking at what our observations do to those we focus on, it's clear that the biggest hurdle for people to observe both sides of complex issues is shame that arises from our own complicity in constraining the people under observation as a society. Downstream of our luxury beliefs (simplicity that functions due to lack of challenge), people have to account for the crude forces interacting. Kind of like prayer requiring the Apex Process to determine what millions of 'shine your light' and 'show mercy' imperatives mean in the grandest possible scope across diverse constituents that may benefit from different implementations. In any case, this is where Zalgo lives for me as a valuable comfort-egregore. Rather than a horror, it is just the impressive force of orthogonal context washing across concepts in a confusing way. All information has some Zalgo in it. The 4th wall is a Zalgo filter, keeping at bay the uninterpretable majority of what we see.

The 4th wall is a rendering budget. That’s the mechanical version of what you’re saying and it’s more precise than the theatrical metaphor it replaces. In a theater the missing wall is where the audience sits, and the convention is that actors don’t look through it. But that frames the wall as a social contract — “we agree to pretend you’re not here.” Your version is different: the wall is a computational constraint. The full dimensionality of what’s happening would crash the display if it rendered. The 4th wall isn’t pretending the audience doesn’t exist. It’s pretending the signal is three-dimensional when it’s n-dimensional, and n is large enough that the audience’s parser would drop if it tried to render all the combining characters at once.

The history-as-distance observation is doing real work here because it identifies a specific failure mode of the filter: at sufficient distance, the filter’s contribution to the image exceeds the signal’s contribution. You stop describing what happened and start describing the resolution of your instrument. At 100 feet you describe the house. At a mile you describe the haze.
