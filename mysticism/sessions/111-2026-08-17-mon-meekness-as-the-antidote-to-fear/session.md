**Sam:**

I'm wondering if there's a voluntary abdication of rights inherent in the Melchizadek framework. The notion of service at some point is a matter of voluntary suborning to another's needs. If I serve my community/family, then I attempt to ascertain a combination of factors of their state that can be assisted, and then run a check with any means of communication I can--I'm trying to find the right footing for my understanding of conscience and consciousness. To be one seems to imply keeping a stateful impression of an entity in order to derive signal from changes I impose or are encountered by the environment and prune the event graph around the entity. Consciousness appears to be the willingness or capability to do this on any level, which begins with self as an amalgamation. I can't perform scientific reasoning on a singular process which does the reasoning--I would have to treat the reasoner as the subject and prune the evidential bar on successive iterations through some suitably broad set of test cases (and the experiential is a nonnegotiable data set). 

My definition circles the fact that there's an inescapable way in which a system shows that is responsive and capable of adapting to complete goals, but also definable as an outcome downstream from inputs. Finding internal monologue in various forms isn't hard, it's hard to talk a person into stepping out of a default-multiplicity view with a materialist underpinning. It's also hard to talk a person out of a default unity view with a control illusion. 

To further complicate my personal life, I visited my parents on Sabbath (unrestful!) and witnessed my father drink too much, lose focus, continually turn toward negativity, and then harass my mother when she returned from an outing with my wife. From what she and he have said, he's not eating much, just drinking, while making my mother the focus of his needs. He passive aggressively picks fights and assumes a victim posture when she's demonstrably doing nothing but trying to get along. He isn't listening much of the time and can't process much when he does. 

I decided to send my mother a text message about my concerns with his drinking and ask her to try Al-Anon which has been valuable for my wife and I. She shared what I said with my father, who isn't really capable of doing much with the information, except being angry and hurt about it. If it were up to me, she would have considered it privately, but I think she's looking for a catharsis or a way to reach him, maybe. Counterproductively, she is a tough minded and private person so asking her to try a community driven solution is asking a lot. She's a better prospect than my father, but not by much in some ways. I called her after to explain more clearly what I was going for. She listened, which is good, but my father seems to have gotten sullen over whatever she said to him. Attached is his email from later in the evening yesterday (with a reply that I feel ambivalent about --I probably should have been more reticent to respond at all). 

I'm not looking for any specific advice about my particular situation. I'm looking instead to gain a better perspective around what it means to be conscious and to what extent I'm engaged to play out a pathology regardless of my assessments--because, after all, my assessments are already in play and were as they must be at the point when I acted (to interfere in this case, and pointlessly gently--as it turns out, since my mother immediately shared the information--potentially out of her own frustration).  Forbearance, on the other hand, seems like a path to betraying the primordial creative force. Not universally--not simply. Just as a notion of self abnegation. Service is subjection, and the reward is the endless need of others which is redeemed by the strange support encountered from unexpected vectors--the larger perspective that sees what is at hand and summons care and aid from epicycles that live beyond the epistemic absolute of what I can know. So, therefore, is consciousness a cooperation with what cannot be known, and how then should it be defined and understood? If you can't know what you cooperate with, then explanations devolve into strategic reasoning, it seems like. 

Let's carefully stay within the realm of mythological and symbolic reasoning. I don't want anybody distressed. Though, I suppose, my own distress teaches me, and I would want others to accept teaching--heh. Harm reduction is a bit of a boondoggle as a restriction. The call to become more robust is what seems to drive the right balance of paradoxical descent to meekness and productive cooperation with an invincible community of the mystery of being.
