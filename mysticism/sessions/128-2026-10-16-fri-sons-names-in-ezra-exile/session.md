I’ve added all 44 Thai consonants as pull request #3. Its check passed, so they go live when you merge it. In the family of scripts, open Thai and tap any letter. You’ll see the word it’s named after, its tone class, its sounds, and the Sanskrit letter it continues.

Thai letter names work like a second version of alef and the ox. ก is “ko kai”, k as in kai, chicken. The difference is that the Thai word came after the letter, as a way to teach it. The picture didn’t come first. A few letters reach back to the Hebrew:

ฏ is named for patak, a goad, the same thing lamed is named for.
อ has no sound of its own and holds the vowel, the way alef does.
ห can also be written silently in front of another letter. There it changes that letter’s tone, so it works as a tone mark.
You were right about the mouth and the water. The same two pictures went on into the alphabet. The water ripple became mem, from mayim, “water.” Pe, whose name means “mouth,” is usually thought to have begun as a picture of a mouth. That’s now part of the Egyptian note too, so Egyptian rn, “name,” has those two Hebrew letters standing behind it.
