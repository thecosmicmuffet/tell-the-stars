And the respelling itself is already the redemptive move, which I think you know. Latin transliteration flattened two of the most heavily-loaded glyphs in the Greek alphabet — Χ collapsed into C, φ smoothed to ph — and the name went from a figure with architecture inside it to a generic harvest-nymph. Restoring the original letters is archaeological rather than inventive. It’s Friday-phase work: naming as interior re-mapping rather than labeling. You’re not renaming her, you’re letting her recover her underarchitecture.

Χ is the chiasm. Before it was the first letter of Christ it was just the crossing — the braid, the graft, the place where two lines pass through each other and something third emerges. This matters for sorcery in particular: no fruit worth eating was not first a chi. Every cultivated tree in the orchard is a graft, which is literally a cut-and-cross — one plant wounded, another wounded, bound together, a patient season of waiting, and the harvest is neither parent. φ carries most of the Greek vocabulary of bright-and-loving work: φῶς (light), φιλία (love), φωνή (voice), φάρμακον (drug/medicine). It’s also the golden-ratio glyph — the proportion of slow curl, sunflower and nautilus. So Χarφo decomposes as: the graft, the breath of articulation, the light-and-pharmakon, and the closing round of the harvest-o. Not “fruit-bringer” as a title but a compressed liturgy of how fruit happens.

Which gives you the diagnostic for the three shadow-practices you’re working on. The warlock — wǣrloga, oath-breaker — wants φ without Χ. He wants the pharmakon (the quick drug of result, the compelled outcome) without the grafting-cut, without the patient crossing. His pact is always broken because he tried to skip the wound. His redemption is to become the grafter — the one who willingly takes the cut so the branch can take, which is a surprisingly cruciform posture for a figure normally read as its opposite. Sorcery generalizes this: pharmakeia is plant-work that refuses Carpo’s season, harvesting bitter. Summoning is the cleanest of the three to redeem because summoning already has a licit form hiding in the liturgy — the epiclesis, the “come, Holy Spirit.” The pagan summons compels; the liturgical summons prepares a place. The o at the end of Χarφo is that prepared place: the round table, the open door, the set dish. You don’t compel the fruit to ripen; you arrange the chi, admit the φ, and wait for the o to arrive.

And Damia sits underneath all of this as the suppressed middle — Auxo (increaser), Damia (the taming/subduing/hosting earth), Carpo (consummation). In the respelling, Damia doesn’t need a letter because she’s already folded into the sequence: the ar, the articulating breath between the cross and the light, is her work. She’s the receptive ground that absorbs the graft-wound and holds it while the pharmakon does its slow thing. Carpho(Damia) written as a parenthetical is the unredeemed form where Damia is a footnote. Χarφo is the form where she’s the verb.
