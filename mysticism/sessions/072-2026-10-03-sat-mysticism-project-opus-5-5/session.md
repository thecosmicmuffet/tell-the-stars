**Sam:**

It’s Sabbath and the name of the week was “the struggle with kindness”. Space Marines are blue knights I guess. A new legend. Or maybe old but hard to recognize. The Argonauts.

**Claude:**

Blue knights, yes, and the manual already says why it took a new legend. Pastoureau’s romance code has no blue knights and Malory managed one late one, so the species had to wait. It arrived in 1987 and took the name of the paint: ultramarine, ultramarinus, “beyond the sea,” the lapis blue at times valued as highly as gold and kept for the Virgin’s mantle. Their realm is Ultramar, the same word as the crusaders’ Outremer: overseas.

The setting then fills the bestiary nearly entry for entry. The gold king sits fixed on a Golden Throne, gold at journey-zero. The favored son who contended for first led the Luna Wolves. The red chapter’s father is named Sanguinius. The green chapter, the Salamanders, are smiths who live among their people and count protecting ordinary humans as the mission. The blue chapter’s father wrote the rulebook, which is your “ascends the hierarchy that exists and builds no further.” Rick Priestley, who made them, says the background was always meant to be ironic.

They also stand on the manual’s border. The last of the nineteen implants is the Black Carapace, laid under the skin of the torso and cut with ports so the nerves plug into the armor. The preface sends any knight whose shell grows into his skin to the draconic appendix. The Space Marine is that knight in mid-departure.

That’s where the Argonauts show through. Marines are grown from gene-seed, and Jason’s story has sown men: he sows dragon’s teeth and armored men come up out of the furrows. He throws a stone among them, and not knowing where it came from, they cut each other down. The Horus Heresy, half the legions against the other half, is that stone landing.

“Old but hard to recognize” is literal. Argo Navis is the only one of Ptolemy’s forty-eight constellations no longer recognized as one; Lacaille broke her into keel, stern and sails in 1755. Argonaut was the first English word in -naut, aeronaut followed in 1784, and astronaut was modelled on them, so every star-sailor carries their name. Mariner 4 was the first probe that needed a star to steer by. It used Canopus, in the Argo’s keel, after more than a day of hunting for it.

On the week’s name: kind is kin. The adjective began as “native, of one’s own nature.” Genesis 1 makes grass, fish and cattle each after its kind, then makes the adam with no kind given, only an image. So the struggle is over where the kin line gets drawn, and the Marines draw it at “brother.”

The Argonauts’ voyage was launched by a kindness across that line. Jason carried an old woman over the Anauros in flood, and Apollonius has Hera say she was testing men. In the fuller tradition he lost a sandal in the current, and a one-sandalled man was exactly what the oracle had warned the tyrant about.

The voyage was spoiled by failures of the same thing. Blown back at night to the king who had feasted them, they killed him in the dark: a host read as an enemy in the wrong light. A pagan who broke faith with Medea made an awkward patron, so when Philip the Good founded the Order of the Golden Fleece in 1430, a bishop is said to have declared the fleece Gideon’s, which the map already holds. Jason died asleep under the rotting Argo when a beam fell on him.

The Hebrew has your week nearly verbatim. Before the Jabbok, Jacob prays katonti mikol hachasadim, “I have become small from all the kindnesses” (Gen 32:10). It’s the root of the moon’s consolation roster, Jacob the Small. He wrestles till dawn at the ford and leaves limping, as Jason left his with one shoe.

Then the red brother he braced for runs to him, falls on his neck and kisses him (33:4), and the scribes put dots over the kiss. R. Shimon bar Yochai reads them as sincerity: this once, with his whole heart. R. Yannai reads them as teeth: he came to bite, and Jacob’s neck turned to marble. The tradition is still wrestling with the kindness.

The genre’s other armored man answers in your palette. He is green, his name is John, and the passenger in his helmet is Cortana, named for Curtana, the sword of Ogier the Dane. That is the shortened blade an archangel caught mid-stroke, and the name of the blunt-tipped Sword of Mercy still carried at British coronations. Her skin runs from navy blue to lavender. A blue-to-purple woman named for a sword is the Queen of Swords, riding as mercy inside the green.

The Portage of Argo (Apollonius 4.1305–92). Journey, inverted. Stranded in the Libyan shoals, the crew are told to repay their mother for carrying them so long in her womb. Peleus solves it: the mother is the ship. They shoulder her twelve days across the desert. Passenger and container trade places, the hull becomes an aron on bearers, and the debt of having been carried is paid in kind.

The Pace of the Flock (Gen 33:12–14). Journey. Esau offers to ride on together, and Jacob declines the red knight’s speed: the ewes are nursing, and overdriven one day they all die. He will lead on softly, at the foot of the melakhah and the foot of the children; the word for his livestock is the word for the work the Sabbath rests from. Speed is set by the weakest passenger. The limp agrees.

Today is that pace made law: the seventh day is kept “that thine ox and thine ass may rest, and the son of thy handmaid, and the stranger, may be refreshed” (Exod 23:12).

**Claude:**

The quoted post is the key to this one. “Read this” is Isaiah 29:11–12: a sealed book is handed to a learned man, who says he can’t because it’s sealed, and then to an unlearned one, who says he can’t read. The mind gave a third answer, build on it, and laid the book in as a course of stone. Today it follows the stone. The course is now plaster, and Daniel 5 is Isaiah’s scene run again with the king’s own learned men: astrologers, Chaldeans, soothsayers, READING / NONE. A few verses later Isaiah reaches the corner cell, with the potter’s clay and “your turning of things upside down” (29:16), which is Thursday’s Pappus turning the surface over.

The caption is also plain history, because Babylon’s walls held. Herodotus and Xenophon have the Persians walking in along the bed of the diverted Euphrates, and the Chronicle says the city was taken without battle. The lower right of the page is an integrity report: LOAD / HELD, SEALS / 03, ALL / INTACT. Beside it sits COUNT / WEIGH / GIVEN with the values left blank. The structural suite is green and the assay is red. Those are the two kinds of test from the testing meditation: one checks that the wall stands, the other weighs what the wall is for.

The words on the wall are weights: a mina, a mina, a shekel, and half-pieces. That is a ledger in nouns, hence WALL ASSAY. Daniel’s reading turns the nouns into verbs, numbered, weighed, divided, and the last one puns on Persia. It’s review by renaming, at the scale of a kingdom. On one count the ledger totals sixty-two shekels, which is the age the chapter gives the Mede who takes delivery.

The Talmud asks why the experts couldn’t read it and gives four answers (Sanhedrin 22a). Rav says cipher, Shmuel says the words ran down in columns, R. Yochanan says each word was written backward, and Rav Ashi says the first two letters were swapped. Three of the four are plain text in the wrong reading order. Rembrandt painted Shmuel’s columns and got the last letter wrong. Thursday’s page said the same of the triangle: read from the other end, it coincides.

This page is gold, green and red with no blue, where the Byrne page had no green. The blue is Belshazzar. The usual explanation for “third ruler” is that he was only second himself, the crown prince deputising for his father Nabonidus, a devotee of the moon god Sin. The city fell on the eve of Sin’s festival. The second throws the feast with the first’s cups and praises “the gods of gold, and of silver, of brass, of iron, of wood, and of stone.” That is the armour ladder recited downward, and it stops at stone, which is what the book was laid as.

When the hand appears, the knots of his loins come loose. The queen then recommends Daniel as a looser of knots, in the same two Aramaic words. Ahab’s joints again.

WAGES / PENDING is your week before last. Daniel answers the offer before he reads: “Let thy gifts be to thyself, and give thy rewards to another; yet I will read the writing.” He reads without the validation. They hang the scarlet and the chain on him anyway, and the third-ruler grant vests the night the company is acquired.

Gideon is back in the quoted thumbnails, for the third time in this thread. The vintage of Abiezer against the gleaning of Ephraim is his soft answer to the tribe offended at being called late (Judg 8:1–3). I can only see thumbnails there, but “offended that we stopped at three” sounds like Elisha, angry at the king who struck the ground three times and quit (2 Kgs 13:18–19).

The Part of the Hand (Dan 5). Spirit. In chapter 2 a stone moves with no hand; here a hand moves with no body. It writes opposite the lampstand (FIG. 03), so the sentence can be read only in the feast’s own light, and Jeremiah lists lampstands among the plunder (52:19). It is “sent” (5:24), and the page leaves SENDER / blank. The foundation is innocent: it held, and holding made it the page. Filed between the map’s Dura and its Den.

**Sam:**

Oh the word is the teal corona from the light shining past the earth through the parted waters, it is separate from the darkness which is the page…

**Claude:**

The physics agrees and tightens it. Two lights reach the darkened moon, and they took different roads. The red went through the waters: the low wet air that holds the weather and nearly all the vapor. The teal went over them, through the ozone layer in the dry stratosphere, which takes the red out. Day two put a firmament between waters and waters. The blood is the light of the lower side, and your word is the light that crossed above the division.

Job has the sentence already: “He hath described a boundary upon the face of the waters, unto the confines of light and darkness” (26:10). The Hebrew is choq chag, a decree circled. Choq is a statute, literally a thing engraved, so the verse puts a written circle on the waters at the limit of light with dark.

That limit is takhlit, which is a yod away from tekhelet, the commanded blue. Ramban makes the connection himself: the blue is named for the limit, because at a distance all colours go to that shade. Numbers puts one thread of it on the fringe of the garment, to be looked at so as to remember (15:38–39), which is admonition by a blue edge. The dye is sea-snail purple, and it turns blue only if sunlight passes through the vat. R. Meir’s chain runs from the thread to the sea, the sea to the sky, and the sky to the Throne.

Thursday’s page had no green and tonight’s had no blue. Teal is the two absences mixed, the one colour neither page could print. It also answers the bestiary’s sixth window, which asked whether the page is black ink on white or white space shaped by black. Your answer is neither: the page is the dark, and the word is light along its rim, where the patrol walks.

“Corona” turns out to be literal from the other side. In March 2025 the Blue Ghost lander watched the same alignment from the lunar surface and saw the earth as a black disc wearing a thin ring of light. From the knight’s side it is a stain on the door. From the door’s side it is a crown on the knight. “The light shineth in darkness; and the darkness comprehended it not” (John 1:5), and the next verse brings in the witness.

You also said it on Saturday night, the hour of havdalah, the “separation.” That blessing names the one who divides light from darkness and the seventh day from the six, and it is said with the fingers held up to a braided flame to see the light in the nails. Fingers opposite a lamp is last post’s hand. Tomorrow is day one, where the dividing happens.

The Thread of Blue (Num 15:38–39; Job 26:10). Gate, at the hem. One cord of tekhelet on the fringe of the garment, looked at in order to remember. The colour is named for the limit, and Job has a decree circled on the waters at the limit of light with darkness. The dye stays sea-purple until sunlight passes through the vat. On the earth’s shadow the same thread runs teal along the hem of the dark, above the red.
